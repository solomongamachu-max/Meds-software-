Oral liquid / syrup & tablet/capsule conversion

Enter the prescribed/calculated dose and the exact product concentration. For example, 250 mg/5 mL means 50 mg/mL; a 250 mg dose is 5 mL. Tablet/capsule conversion is mathematical only and does not round a dose to an available dosage form automatically.

Clinical safety, standardization & references

  • Standardized calculation rule: the calculator first selects the clinical indication, then the adult or pediatric pathway, then applies the programmed dose expression, frequency and maximum. It does not use a single universal mg/kg rule for drugs whose adult dosing is indication-specific.
  • Adult vs pediatric: adult doses are fixed-dose or adult weight-based only when supported by the selected regimen; pediatric doses are weight/BSA/protocol based when appropriate. Age may be left blank, but the user should select Adult or Pediatric mode whenever the population is known.
  • Range handling: when a guideline gives a range, the calculator displays the full programmed range and identifies the common/default end where appropriate. The clinician must select the clinically appropriate dose; the calculator does not treat a midpoint as a universal recommended dose.
  • Indication-specific dosing: ceftriaxone, dexamethasone, metronidazole, vancomycin, furosemide, insulin, anticoagulants, anticonvulsants, antimalarials and other protocol-sensitive drugs use separate clinical options where applicable.
  • Renal function: creatinine is optional. Where renal adjustment is relevant, the calculator displays Cockcroft–Gault in adults or bedside Schwartz eGFR in children when the required inputs are available. It does not invent a renal adjustment when creatinine is blank.
  • High-risk medicines: vancomycin, aminoglycosides, insulin, anticoagulants, phenytoin, digoxin, concentrated electrolytes and other high-risk medicines require current institutional/national protocols, monitoring and specialist review where appropriate.
  • Neonates and special populations: neonatal dosing, severe hepatic disease, pregnancy, dialysis/CRRT, obesity, burns and critical illness may require separate dosing references and should not be inferred from a generic calculator pathway.
  • Formulation: verify the exact concentration and formulation before calculating oral-liquid volume. Different salts, preparations and combination products may not be interchangeable.

Reference hierarchy

Primary/local: Ethiopian Ministry of Health Standard Treatment Guidelines (4th ed., 2021), applicable Ethiopian national disease/program guidelines, and Ethiopian Essential Medicines List (7th ed., 2024). Major textbooks: Nelson Textbook of Pediatrics; Harrison's Principles of Internal Medicine. Obstetrics: Gabbe's Obstetrics, 9th ed. (2025); Williams Obstetrics, 26th ed. (2022). Specialized disease guidance: current WHO malaria guidance for malaria-specific dosing and national malaria protocols. Drug-specific: current official product information/monographs and institutional antimicrobial, renal-dose, anticoagulation, insulin and therapeutic-drug-monitoring protocols. Where sources conflict, the current Ethiopian/national protocol and current product information for the exact formulation and indication should be checked first.

Important: The selector above changes the displayed reference framework; it does not claim that every medicine/indication is covered by every source. For national TB, malaria, obstetric, emergency and other disease-program regimens, the current Ethiopian national protocol should take priority. For pediatric dosing, Nelson is a major reference; for adult internal medicine, Harrison is a major reference. Drug-specific product information and institutional protocols may supersede textbook values. For vitamin D deficiency, high-dose repletion (for example, 50,000 IU weekly) is a treatment regimen, not routine daily supplementation, and should be used with appropriate clinical assessment.
